<p>On your turn, you can move a distance up to your &amp;Reference[Speed] and take one action. You decide whether to move first or take your action first.</p><p>The main actions you can take are listed in the @UUID[JournalEntry.hotbPlayGuide000.JournalEntryPage.OLpDZ0dVH5nUjjee]{Actions} section. A character’s<strong> </strong>and a creature’s character sheets also list action options. The @UUID[JournalEntry.hotbPlayGuide000.JournalEntryPage.SVX0uYx2OeHm9pR3]{Movement and Position} section gives the rules for movement.</p><p><em><strong>Communicating.</strong></em> You can communicate however you are able—through brief utterances and gestures—as you take your turn. Doing so uses neither your action nor your move.</p><p><em><strong>Interacting with Things.</strong></em> You can interact with one object or feature of the environment for free, during either your move or your action. For example, you could open a door during your move as you stride toward a foe.</p><p>If you want to interact with a second object, you need to take the &amp;Reference[Utilize] action. Some magic items and other special objects always require an action to use, as stated in their descriptions.</p><p><em><strong>Doing Nothing on Your Turn.</strong></em> You can forgo moving, taking an action, or doing anything at all on your turn. If you can’t decide what to do, consider taking the &amp;Reference[Dodge] action.</p>
